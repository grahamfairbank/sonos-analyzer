What these readings mean

Noise floor

The background radio noise where the speaker sits, like the hum in a room before anyone speaks. The quieter it is, the easier it is for the speaker to hear your Wi-Fi. Readings are in dBm and are negative: lower (more negative) is quieter and better. Nearby electronics and other wireless gear raise it.

Some older models also report an interference level from 0 to 9: the radio lowers its own sensitivity when it detects interference, and 0 means it's at full sensitivity. As in Sonos's own matrix, a high level lowers the rating. Hover a reading to see it.

Quiet: −95 or lowerSome noise: −90 to −94Noisy: −85 to −89Very noisy: above −85

Speaker-to-speaker signal

How loudly each speaker's radio hears another, as the speakers report it. Sonos doesn't give a unit for this score; higher is better. It describes how the speakers are placed relative to each other.

Strong: 45 and upFair: 30–44Weak: 18–29Poor: under 18

PHY errors per second

Radio signals the speaker picked up but couldn't make sense of, like catching garbled words in a busy room. Every radio hears some, so small numbers are normal: tens or a few hundred per second is everyday background. Only very large numbers, far above your other speakers, point to interference or a weak spot. "Not in use" means the speaker is wired and its radio isn't carrying audio, so the count doesn't affect playback.

Normal: under 500/sHigh: 500/s or moreVery high: far above the rest of your speakers (flagged in Findings)

Network errors

The share of network packets that arrived damaged on the speaker's connection: its Ethernet port when wired, otherwise its wireless link. On a cable this should be zero or very close to it; errors there usually mean a damaged cable or a bad switch port. Measured since the previous scan when there is one, otherwise since the speaker last restarted; under 10,000 packets it shows "Too few packets" instead of a rating.

None or Normal: under 0.01%High: 0.01–0.1%Very high: 0.1% or more

The noise floor and signal bands and colors match the network matrix built into Sonos speakers. Sonos doesn't grade PHY errors; those bands are this tool's guide.
